10. Describe your Solution:
Rental fleets lose money in three quiet ways: equipment goes unaccounted for, sits idle without anyone noticing, or gets returned later than needed. Citadel tracks every rented asset through its full lifecycle — check-out, assignment, usage logging, check-in — and continuously turns that data into decision support: alerts for overdue or missing-information equipment, anomaly detection for idle or misused assets, demand forecasting by equipment type and site, and a capacity-aware optimization signal that compares an asset's actual usage pace against its typical workload to flag likely early-return or reassignment candidates. Every signal comes with a stated reason and a recommended action, across three purpose-built views — Customer, Dealer, and Caterpillar Admin — so the system moves from tracking equipment to supporting the decision of what to do about it.

11. Tools & Technology used:
React (Vite) frontend, Node.js/Express backend (routes → controller → service → repository layering, no ORM), PostgreSQL with parameterized SQL, Zod for input validation, Node's built-in test runner with Supertest for automated testing. Deployment pipeline configured for Vercel (frontend), Render (backend), and Neon (managed PostgreSQL), auto-deploying from GitHub.

12. How are you planning to use AI in building your solution?
Two distinct uses. In development, AI (Claude) is used as an engineering assistant under a documented plan → implement → test → verify → document process — every change is run and checked against real code and real data before being accepted. Inside the product, we use explainable, rule- and threshold-based analytics rather than a black-box model — idle-ratio, overdue, demand-trend, and capacity thresholds are grounded in cited research and validated against real seeded data, and every recommendation traces back to the specific rule that produced it. At our current data volume this is more defensible and more trustworthy than a trained model would be.

13. How is your team approaching this problem?
We studied the problem statement and Caterpillar's own sample dataset closely, wrote a full requirements-traceability document before writing product code, then built backend analytics against real seeded operational data before layering UI on top — every stage run and verified live, not just reviewed as code. Partway through Day 1, after the core system was complete and verified, we made a deliberate scope decision to extend it with three distinct role experiences and a capacity-aware optimization feature, and applied the same discipline to that extension: plan, implement, test, review, fix, verify, before calling it done.

14. Key Features and Unique Selling Point of your Product:
Our differentiator is capacity-aware rental optimization: for each active rental, we compare its observed daily usage rate against an assumed equipment-type capacity and a real historical "typical workload" baseline (the median usage of past rentals of the same type that fell in a healthy utilization band), and turn that into an estimated completion window — shown as a range, with every assumption stated, never a guarantee — surfaced as a recommendation to investigate early return or reassignment. This sits alongside full lifecycle tracking, alerts, anomaly detection, and demand forecasting, and is presented differently to each of three role experiences: Customer (equipment discovery and self-service rentals), Dealer (the operational Control Tower and Asset Dashboard), and Caterpillar Admin (fleet-wide utilization, capacity, and recommendations). In one line: Citadel doesn't just track where equipment is — it estimates whether it's still needed, and says so honestly when the data isn't there yet.
